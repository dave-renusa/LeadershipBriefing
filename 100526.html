RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 57 · Monday, October 5, 2026

Good morning, team.

The second vote is where a fight gets decided. Iberville Parish, Louisiana, had already rejected Entergy's 200-megawatt solar farm once, and on Sept. 30 the council voted 12-1 to stand by that denial, a far wider margin than the earlier 7-4 rejection. Out West, Kern County supervisors approved Terra-Gen's 600-megawatt Galaxy Solar and 4-gigawatt-hour storage project on 3,519 acres, while in the Los Angeles suburbs one city council approved a battery its own planning commission had rejected and another pulled its vote from the agenda. This week's calendar is full of boards deciding whether to say yes a second time or no a second time.

Must Read

Twelve to One, Second Time Around

Entergy's Cypress Harvest Solar would put 200 megawatts on a 1,600-acre tract of former sugarcane fields along Louisiana Highway 75, about five miles from the Plaquemine city limits. The Iberville Parish Council first rejected it 7-4, with one member absent, in front of an overflow crowd that had spectators watching from a screen in the corridor. On Sept. 30 the council returned to the question and voted 12-1 to stand by its denial letter on the permit.

The applicant here is a regulated utility, not a merchant developer, and the council held anyway. Residents had been organizing against the project since at least July, when some Iberville Parish residents spoke out against the Highway 75 plan. By the end, the 7-4 split had become a near-unanimous 12-1.

A denial reaffirmed by a wider margin is a harder thing to unwind than a narrow first vote. Whatever the next move is, a challenge or a reapplication, it now starts from a council that has twice declined and the second time by a landslide.

The Takeaway

Treat the first vote as a draft of the record, not the finish line. A narrow denial invites a rematch, and the rematch in Iberville widened the margin from 7-4 to 12-1. On any file where a board has voted no once, the work between votes is the work: identify the members who were soft, find out what they needed to hear and did not, and have the answer on the record before the item comes back. On files that have not had a first vote, assume the second one is the one that counts.

Also On Our Radar
  • Galaxy Approved: The Kern County Board of Supervisors approved Terra-Gen's Galaxy Solar and Storage project on Sept. 29, 600 megawatts of solar with a 4-gigawatt-hour battery on about 3,519 acres across 184 private parcels near California City. The county projects roughly $16.1 million in first-year property taxes and $120 million over 35 years, with about 650 workers at peak construction under a local union labor agreement. Joshua trees on the site will be moved to a tree sanctuary in Los Angeles County.
  • Approved Over the Planning Commission: The Chino City Council approved the Dirac Energy battery storage facility 4-1 after the planning commission denied it 4-3 in July. Residents say they are preparing to fight on, pointing to the hazardous lithium-ion fire in nearby La Verne that forced hundreds from their homes.
  • Pulled From the Agenda: The Covina City Council had been set to vote on a 110-megawatt battery storage system on 3.4 acres in a mixed business and residential area, but the item was pulled with no new date. The planning commission voted unanimously against the project in June after hundreds of residents turned out.
  • Four Creeks, Final Stretch: The Peoria County Zoning Board of Appeals scheduled hearing days through Oct. 1 on Repsol's Four Creeks Wind, about 113 turbines and up to 663 megawatts across 27,113 acres in Brimfield, Millbrook, Princeville and Jubilee townships. The board's recommendation on the special use permit then goes to the full County Board for a final vote. Repsol's witnesses have included landowners who changed their minds after meeting with the company, citing school-district tax benefits.
  • Rules Rewritten After the Award: The Iowa Farmers Union, the Iowa Solar Energy Trade Association and several co-plaintiffs sued USDA over changes to the Rural Energy for America Program, saying the revisions made "almost all solar projects ineligible" and were applied retroactively to projects already selected and under construction. Chad Hughes of the Environmental Law & Policy Center said farmers were denied under "rules that did not exist when you applied." A separate update makes solar on farmland ineligible for the program's grants, which historically covered 25% of qualifying costs.
  • Portfolio Changes Hands: Exus Renewables North America acquired a 715-MWp solar portfolio from ibV Energy Partners: Maple Grove Solar in Barron County, Wisconsin (310 MWp) and Bayou Teche (125 MWp) and Bayou Chicot (280 MWp) in Louisiana. Exus says it will advance the projects through their next stages of development, and it is buying where it sees potential to interconnect quickly.
On the Frontlines
  • LAHwy. 75 Holds: Iberville Parish's 12-1 vote to stand by its denial of Cypress Harvest Solar lands in a state where Exus just bought two other Louisiana solar projects in St. Mary and Evangeline parishes. Parish by parish, the room decides.
  • COFinished in Weld: ContourGlobal completed the 324-megawatt Black Hollow Sun near Severance for Platte River Power Authority, the largest solar project in northern Colorado. Phase 2 finished Oct. 1, and the utility's CEO described the effort as years of commitment since selection began in 2019.
  • ILOne More Year in Grundy: The Grundy County land use committee granted a one-year extension to a 4.9-megawatt solar farm on 30 acres, moving its construction deadline from Oct. 14, 2026 to Oct. 14, 2027. The full County Board votes Oct. 13. The development director said there have been "no issues" and the developer has been responsive; it has an Illinois Shines grant and a building permit application in.
  • MICold Creek Switches On: DTE's Cold Creek Solar Park is now operational east of Coldwater.
  • NCOne Array, More Ground-Mounts: A 680-kilowatt installation at Sisters of Mercy in Belmont opened the city to more approved ground-mount systems, spurring local zoning modifications. A small project changed a code, which is how most local solar rules get written.
  • CAMoss Landing, Again: Vistra's Moss Landing battery caught fire a second time during decommissioning, following the catastrophic 2025 incident. Expect it in the first slide of every opposition deck against storage this fall.
  • RILast Turbine Up: Revolution Wind, a 704-megawatt offshore project near Rhode Island, installed its last turbine despite federal political opposition.
  • TXClosing on California: Texas solar capacity is approaching California's levels as the state rapidly expands both generation and storage.
  • USSeven Plants, $140 Million: Enel completed the purchase of seven operating solar plants totaling 270 megawatts from a U.S. utility for $140 million.

Iberville said no twice and the second time was nearly unanimous, Chino overrode its own planning commission, and Covina blinked. The votes to watch this week are the repeat ones. For every file where a board has already voted once, write down who moved, who didn't, and what the next room needs to hear.
