RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 62 · Monday, October 19, 2026

Good morning.

This week's biggest solar ruling came out of a contract case, not a zoning hearing. In Ottawa County, Michigan, a judge gave RWE possession of 562 acres of a farm family's land because option notices from a 2020 agreement arrived on time. The family's argument was that they thought they had signed up for a study. In Copake, New York, the state's siting office denied a 42-megawatt solar farm its own staff had twice recommended, after a decade of local opposition. Both stories show how much depends on the paperwork. What a landowner signed, and what an applicant filed on wetlands, decided these cases more than anything said at a hearing.

Must Read

Signed, Sealed, Delivered

Ottawa County Circuit Judge Jon Hulsing ruled for RWE Solar Development in its dispute with the Smallegan family over about 562 acres, roughly 30 percent of the proposed 1,900-acre Silver Maple Solar project in Zeeland and Jamestown townships. The family signed option agreements in November 2020 that gave RWE five years to study the land and the right to exercise lease options. The deadline was Nov. 19, 2025. RWE testified it sent the notices by FedEx on Nov. 13. The judge ruled delivery was complete on Nov. 16. He called the family's testimony that the deal was only a five-year study “not credible,” and he rejected their claims of unconscionability and duress. He also noted that Patricia Smallegan had accepted more than $282,000 in payments after RWE exercised the option.

The order goes well beyond the land itself. RWE now holds exclusive possession and may remove crops. The family must end farming and hunting permissions, and while the agreements stand, the family is barred from participating in administrative, legislative or judicial proceedings opposing the project. RWE can also seek attorney fees. The family's lawyer says an appeal is under consideration. A Zeeland Township resident called the ruling “a heavy blow to farmers and agriculture in the state of Michigan.”

The 200-megawatt project still needs a state permit. Under Public Act 233 of 2023, siting for large solar, wind and storage moved to the Michigan Public Service Commission, and an administrative law judge suspended all case dates in July while RWE evaluated a Court of Appeals decision. That decision broadened who counts as an “affected local unit,” which could let Ottawa County intervene, and the state process is expected to run into 2027. Zeeland Charter Township has separately sued Silver Maple PV and RWE, asking a court to declare PA 233 unconstitutional under Article 7, Section 29 of the Michigan Constitution.

The same law has produced a very different result elsewhere. In August, the commission approved a siting settlement for the 90-megawatt Acceleration Solar project in Ingham County. It was the first project to move forward under PA 233, with host community payments of at least $2,000 per megawatt. In Ingham County the dispute ended in a negotiated settlement. In Ottawa County it is being fought out in three venues: a contract case, a constitutional suit, and a stalled state docket.

The Takeaway

A landowner who says they did not understand what they signed is a problem, even when the developer wins the case. Ottawa County now has a farm family that has been barred from the land and from opposing the project, which gives every opposition group in the state a story to tell. For any client with option agreements, find out before the hearing whether each landowner still understands the deal and still supports it, and fix any gaps in person. The Ingham County settlement shows the alternative: put agreed host payments on the record and the local fight ends.

Also On Our Radar
  • Copake Wins the Long Game: New York's Office of Renewable Energy Siting on Oct. 14 denied Hecate Energy's 42-megawatt Shepherd's Run on 215 acres of Columbia County farmland, finding the application did not fully address wetlands impacts or floodplain protections. Sensible Solar for Rural New York had fought the project for about a decade, and federal agriculture and EPA officials wrote to the governor in June questioning farmland solar. Hecate says ORES staff twice recommended approval and is weighing legal options.
  • Until December, Then: Texas regulators voted Oct. 15 to extend their time to consider rehearing motions on Oncor's 390-mile Dinosaur-to-Drill Hole 765-kV line to the 100-day legal maximum, past the Oct. 22 date when the motions would otherwise have been overruled. American Stewards of Liberty, two pipeline companies and the Middle Trinity Groundwater Conservation District asked for rehearing. Rehearing motions are also pending on the Howard-to-Solstice line, and the Bell County East-to-Big Hill segment is back before the administrative judges.
  • Ocean City's Turn: The federal government asked U.S. District Judge Stephanie Gallagher in Baltimore to send US Wind's construction permit back to Interior for reconsideration “without vacatur,” so the permit would stay in place during the review. The Town of Ocean City brought the original suit nearly two years ago. US Wind says it is “confident that a court will uphold” its permits. The Revolution Wind remand request was filed the week before.
  • 6,500 and Counting: Valley Link has set 12 more open houses for Nov. 4–19 on Valley North, a 765-kV line from the John Amos plant in Putnam County, West Virginia toward Frederick County, Maryland, with sessions in Tucker and Gilmer counties and in Frederick County, Virginia. More than 6,500 people attended the first round. The developer says opponents feel the line will not benefit them, and it points to about $14 million a year in property taxes.
  • Five Months to Five-Nothing: Cherokee County, Iowa adopted an indefinite countywide battery moratorium in March, then approved a battery ordinance 5-0 on Aug. 26. The moratorium had put at risk a family's 30-megawatt solar-and-grazing project with a 25-megawatt, 100-megawatt-hour battery. The family chose to persuade the board rather than sue. A supervisor made the tax case, $60,000 to $80,000 a year, an Iowa State researcher held safety workshops, and the fire chief addressed concerns directly.
On the Frontlines
  • VAGibson Solar, Smaller and Deferred: King George County's Planning Commission on Oct. 13 unanimously deferred its recommendation on Gibson Solar to an undetermined date. Supervisors denied the project in July 2025, and it has returned on a smaller 640-acre footprint paired with a livestock operation.
  • NYNorth Elba Hits Pause: The town approved a one-year moratorium on battery storage after two solar applications in Ray Brook, about 25 megawatts each with a battery, exposed gaps in its zoning. Champlain and Ticonderoga have similar pauses.
  • MI360 Megawatts Through the Commission: The Public Service Commission on Oct. 15 approved agreements for three DTE solar projects totaling 360 megawatts, including the 185-megawatt Peatville Solar in Sanilac County, at an estimated $76 per megawatt-hour. The approval covers supply and service contracts, not construction permits.
  • MIHuron County Holds the Line: Next door in the Thumb, Huron County is challenging Ranger Power's 150-megawatt Pinesap Solar, cutting a planned countywide solar acreage cap from 9,000 to 5,000 acres (still under legal review), and holding a moratorium on new utility-scale solar and battery permits through Dec. 29.
  • WYSixteen Miles Through South Cheyenne: Residents pushed back at a hearing on a 16-mile line carrying power from Enyo's Weld County-approved Chalk Bluffs Wind into Cheyenne, citing views, property values, falling ice and inadequate notice. The county says it has no legal role in the interstate line. Its planning commission votes on the project's Laramie County turbines Nov. 12, and commissioners decide Dec. 1.
  • TX$1,500 a Megawatt in Bowie County: Commissioners scheduled an Oct. 13 hearing and vote on a 10-year abatement for TotalEnergies' Red Barn Solar, a 200-megawatt solar plant with a 100-megawatt battery on about 3,400 acres. The draft requires payments of $1,500 per megawatt a year, about $300,000, which is above the company's own offer of $194,492.
  • AZSix Named, Seven Counted: An Arizona's Family review counts seven communities that ban or restrict large solar and wind, naming Yavapai, Mohave and Navajo counties and Chino Valley, Coolidge and Eloy. The state solar association describes hostile meetings, some moved online over safety concerns.
  • VADominion Doubles Down on Storage: Dominion's latest long-term plan calls for 21 percent more solar and 76 percent more battery storage than last year's update, and projects bills rising 2.26 percent a year over 20 years, from $180.31 to $283.65 for a benchmark 1,000-kilowatt-hour month.
  • VAStafford Approves One, Rethinks the Rest: Supervisors approved the Accokeek Data Center on Oct. 7 and will take up removing data centers from the technology-zone incentive program, and from by-right use in the M-2 district, at an Oct. 27 work session.
  • VANo More NDAs, Locally: Fredericksburg City Council on Oct. 13 agreed to lobby for legislation extending the governor's September executive order banning nondisclosure agreements in data center deals to local governments.
  • NCDavidson Tries Again Oct. 26: After deadlocking 3-3, Davidson County commissioners voted 4-2 to put a four-month data center moratorium on the Oct. 26 agenda. The chair, who opposes it, warns the projects and their revenue would go to neighboring counties.

In Ottawa County the developer won its case, and a farm family that says it never understood the deal is now the face of the project. Before any client's hearing, confirm that every landowner who signed still understands the agreement and still supports it.
