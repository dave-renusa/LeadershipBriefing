RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 60 · Monday, October 12, 2026

Happy Monday, all.

Three different rooms are arguing the same question this week: who gets the last word on a solar farm. In Illinois, two appeals courts have read the same state statute in opposite ways, one saying counties must issue permits to projects that meet state standards and one saying courts cannot make them. In Iowa, Cerro Gordo County is suing the state utilities board for letting a 500-megawatt project skip local zoning, and in Louisa County, Virginia, planners voted 7-0 to recommend denying a 200-megawatt battery on the strength of the county's own comprehensive plan. The venue keeps shifting, and so does who is willing to defend the local no.

Must Read

“Shall Be Approved,” Maybe

Illinois's 2023 solar amendments to the Counties Code say a special use permit for a solar farm “shall be approved” if it meets the state's siting standards. Two appellate districts have now read that sentence differently. The Third District, in Equity Solar Illinois v. County of Grundy, affirmed an order requiring a county to issue a permit to applicants who met the statewide standards, and held that mandamus was the right tool because counties may be less restrictive than the state but must approve what meets the floor. It binds the Third District, which covers Chicago's south suburbs and neighboring counties.

The Fourth District went the other way. In Tate Road Solar 1, LLC v. County of Winnebago, decided June 2, it held that courts cannot compel a county to issue the permit even when a project meets the state standards, reasoning that “shall be approved” applies only where the project also satisfies whatever subjective standards the county imposed. Grundy County's petition asking the Illinois Supreme Court to review Equity Solar is pending, per that same analysis.

The practical effect is already visible. A Will County circuit court applied Equity Solar and ordered the county to grant special use permits for six solar projects it had denied. Meanwhile, in Madison County, the board voted by a slim margin to postpone a decision on Summit Ridge Energy's Troy-area solar farm to its October meeting after an amended resolution arrived hours before the September session, while a neighborhood coalition asks for a denial over size, location and elevation.

Which district a project sits in now matters as much as what is in the application. A developer in the Third District can litigate a denial with real leverage. A developer in the Fourth District cannot compel anything and is back to persuading a board. Until the Supreme Court acts, every Illinois county board is deciding with one eye on which appellate court would hear the appeal.

The Takeaway

Build the file as if a court will read it, and map which district a client's county sits in before the hearing, not after the denial. Where the Third District rule applies, the record should show the project meeting the state standards line by line, because that is the showing a judge will check. Where the Fourth District rule applies, the file is only as strong as the board's willingness to say yes, which makes the work in the room, not the brief, the deciding factor.

Also On Our Radar
  • Palouse Detour: Steelhead Americas' 45-turbine Harvest Hills Wind in Whitman County, Washington is in pre-application with the state Energy Facility Site Evaluation Council, which can override county rules. The county's commissioners adopted new commercial wind rules 2-1, including a seven-mile buffer around Kamiak Butte, which the developer said functionally prohibits local permitting. Nimiipuu Protecting the Environment, run by Nez Perce tribal members, wrote to the governor in support; Save the Palouse opposes, citing soils, roads and landscape.
  • Seven to Nothing on Harris Creek Road: The Louisa County, Virginia Planning Commission voted 7-0 to recommend that supervisors deny East Point Energy's Ava Energy Center, a battery of up to 200 megawatts on part of a 92-acre agricultural site. Commissioners cited conversion of farmland to industrial use, nonconformity with the county's 2040 Comprehensive Plan, fire risk and thin community benefits. East Point offered to defer and asked to negotiate a siting agreement; no board hearing has been scheduled.
  • The County Sues the State: Cerro Gordo County, Iowa filed in Polk County District Court to reverse the Iowa Utilities Commission's certificate for Ranger Power's 500-megawatt River City solar project. The commission issued it in April without requiring county zoning permits. The developer called county rules a “de facto moratorium”; the county says River City never applied for a permit and that it has never denied one for a generating project. The suit says the commission applied an old rule over state law.
  • Five Thousand Acres, One Judge: A Wagoner County, Oklahoma judge is expected to rule in October or November on NextEra's Persica Solar, a 5,000-acre, $336 million array near Porter that commissioners denied. Persica calls the county's reasons speculation and cites about $31 million in ad valorem taxes, most of it for Porter schools. The county says solar is a conditional use and Persica did not carry its burden. The judge set findings of fact due before he rules.
  • Injunction, Then Trial: The bench trial over Douglas County, Kansas's approval of the 159-megawatt Kansas Sky Energy Center is set to begin at the end of November at the earliest. Grant Township and neighboring property owners sued in 2024, and a temporary injunction has held construction off since December of that year. In June the judge ruled that planning commissioners can be subpoenaed.
On the Frontlines
  • KSFinney County Paperwork: The Holcomb–Garden City–Finney County Area Planning Commission recommended approval of the 330-megawatt Lone Bison Solar special use permit on March 19, and the county's notice lists a development agreement, road-use agreement, decommissioning agreement and community benefit agreement as required before construction. A separate notice covers a Planning Commission hearing on a 600-megawatt natural gas facility requested by a Triple Oak Power subsidiary.
  • ILSix Permits by Order: The Will County ruling applying Equity Solar granted summary judgment to all plaintiffs and ordered the county to approve their special use ordinances.
  • OH52.9 Percent: Richland County voters upheld the commissioners' ban on industrial-scale wind and solar, 12,189 to 10,853, in a May 5 referendum. It was the second test in Ohio of the 2021 law that lets local governments block renewables; NRDC and Ohio Citizen Action spent heavily for repeal.
  • AZWhite Tank Money: GridStor secured $220 million in tax equity bridge, construction and term debt for its 100-megawatt, 400-megawatt-hour White Tank battery, expected in service in the first half of 2027.
  • NMIntent, Not Approval: The Torrance County Commission unanimously adopted a resolution declaring its intent to issue up to $150 million in industrial revenue bonds for Exus Renewables' 60-megawatt Britton Storage battery on 7.69 acres inside an existing solar site. Payments in lieu of taxes would run $2,800 per megawatt for five years, about $168,000 a year; a commissioner asked whether the rate can be negotiated. The earliest public hearing is likely Nov. 11.
  • WYFive Minutes in Crook County: Commissioners unanimously approved Powder River Energy's LaBelle Prairie project in about five minutes: a 1.2-megawatt array of roughly 2,100 panels plus a 5-megawatt, four-hour battery near Moorcroft. No one spoke for or against, though the vice-chair said many residents had called to complain about solar and he was worried about battery fires. The co-op had held town halls.
  • VAA Pause That Is Not a Moratorium: Loudoun County supervisors voted Sept. 15 to pause new data center applications for up to 12 months, the state-law maximum, 7-1-1 by the county's tally. The county attorney advised that a moratorium is not legal, so the board structured it as deferral of legislative applications. A formal resolution is slated for an Oct. 20 business meeting, though some outlets report Oct. 13.
  • WAFour Months to Write the Rules: After a 3-2 party-line split on a nine-month moratorium, Spokane County commissioners unanimously approved a four-month pause on data center permits through Dec. 15, extendable, while the Planning Commission runs a zoning text amendment. A draft would require noise and vibration studies, water limits and emergency response plans.
  • NC120 Days in Columbus County: Commissioners scheduled a public hearing for Oct. 5 on a 120-day pause on approvals for data centers and other high-intensity computing in unincorporated areas. Whiteville, Southport and Boiling Spring Lakes have already adopted temporary moratoriums.
  • ILTroy Waits a Month: Madison County's vote on Summit Ridge Energy's solar farm is on the board's October agenda after the September postponement; the Residents of Spring Valley Road Coalition wants it denied.

A statute can say “shall be approved” and still mean different things two districts apart. For every file in a county that has said no, find out which court would hear the appeal, and what it has already held, before the next hearing.
