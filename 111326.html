RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 71 · Friday, November 13, 2026

Friday morning, team.

This week, more of the deciding happened above the county board. A Mississippi appeals court split over whether Hinds County had to write down its reasons for approving a 5,800-acre solar farm, and the approval survived. An Iowa developer turned down by a county board has gone to the state utilities commission instead. In federal court, the Pentagon said eight employees are working a queue of about 400 onshore wind projects. Local governments are answering with tools a court is less likely to overturn: acreage caps in Indiana, a 1,000-foot battery setback in Iowa, and short moratoriums timed to fit notice rules. The plays below show both sides of that, and which side writes the record first.

Also On Our Radar
  • Found in the Findings: The Mississippi Court of Appeals upheld the Hinds County Board of Supervisors' 2024 approval of two use permits for Soul City Energy's solar and storage project in western Hinds County. A resident argued the board erred by issuing no written findings. The majority held the findings were incorporated by reference to the application and supplemental materials, and noted the developer revised its plan and accepted conditions. Four judges dissented and would have sent it back. An approval can be attacked on its paperwork just as a denial can.
  • Over the County's Head: After Louisa County, Iowa's Board of Adjustment voted 4-1 in September to deny a special-use permit for Southpaw Solar near Letts, the developer asked the Iowa Utilities Commission for a state certificate. The commission holds an informational meeting Nov. 30 in Columbus Junction. Iowa law lets the commission approve qualifying projects that don't fully meet county zoning, and a local state representative opposes the request. This is the route to watch for any Iowa client facing a county no.
  • Carver's Clock Runs Out: Isle of Wight County, Virginia's planning commission voted 7-2 on Nov. 10 to recommend denying a one-year extension of the expired permit for the 91-megawatt Carver Solar farm. Commissioners noted the county has already passed its own limit on prime farm soils used for solar. Supervisors take it up Dec. 10. An approved project whose permit lapses goes back in front of the board.
  • Eight People, 400 Projects: In a Nov. 3 filing in Oregon federal court, the Defense Department said it has resumed reviewing wind permits under an August injunction. It also said action on a queue of about 400 onshore wind projects rests with eight federal employees. American Clean Power says substantive review has not restarted, and that one project's turbine delivery has slipped past a federal tax-credit deadline. Plaintiffs reply Nov. 17.
  • Batteries on the Coal Pile: Florida Power & Light commissioned its Lansing Smith battery in Bay County: 400 megawatts for up to four hours, or 1,600 megawatt-hours. It is the utility's largest battery by storage capacity and sits on land that was a coal storage area. FPL's battery fleet now totals nearly 2 gigawatts.
  • Fifty Kilowatts, Full Stop: Boulder County, Colorado commissioners voted unanimously to cap data centers at 50 kilowatts in unincorporated areas. That ends their moratorium and effectively bars large facilities. The planning commission had proposed 75 kilowatts, and the cap dropped after an executive session on legal concerns. Setting a capacity limit low enough to work as a ban gets the same result as a ban.
On the Frontlines
  • INCapped, Then Tabled: Dearborn County's plan commission tabled its solar-rules recommendation at a Nov. 12 special meeting after a heated hearing. In the debate it settled on a 500-acre cap per project and a 1,500-acre cap countywide. The county's one-year moratorium dates from February.
  • IAA Thousand Feet From the Door: Greene County published zoning amendments covering wind, solar, battery storage and data centers. Commercial batteries need a conditional use and a 1,000-foot setback from dwellings. Towers up to 700 feet may be conditionally permitted in agricultural districts. Turbines are limited to 30 hours of shadow flicker a year on non-participating buildings.
  • IAHenry Holds Through December: Henry County's temporary moratorium on commercial solar and battery storage runs through Dec. 31. It is unclear how the state's power to approve projects that don't fully meet county zoning would apply to it.
  • VAKilmarnock Says No, Council Next: Kilmarnock's planning commission unanimously recommended denying the rezoning and permit for a 20-acre solar facility on Nov. 12. Opponents cited property values and the loss of land that could be used for housing. Town council holds hearings and votes Dec. 14.
  • KYSilicon Ranch Waits: Madison County's Board of Adjustments has set no new date for a vote on Silicon Ranch's 53-MW, roughly 830-acre solar farm off Crooksville Road, after postponing its October vote. The board's Nov. 19 meeting is canceled.
  • IAPage Makes It Final: Page County supervisors passed the amended solar ordinance 2-1 on Nov. 10 and waived the third reading, so it takes effect on publication. The dissenting supervisor faulted the board for not keeping the 2024 ordinance its planning and zoning commission had recommended.
  • ILKing Creek Clears: Bureau County's board approved King Creek Solar, a 5-MW project on 39 acres in rural Mineral, with power sold to Ameren.
  • NYMore Time for Bulk Storage: Con Edison asked state regulators to extend a key deadline for transmission-connected bulk storage and approve a new dispatch program for distribution-level batteries. New York utilities have also proposed a framework to develop and own up to 1 GW of land-based renewables a year.
  • NCEighteen Months in Greensboro: Guilford County commissioners unanimously approved an 18-month pause on data center development, replacing a 180-day moratorium.
  • INWarrick Tables Its Rules: Warrick County commissioners unanimously tabled proposed data center rules after residents spoke, a week after the area plan commission sent the draft over with an unfavorable recommendation. Some residents asked for another six-month pause.
Plays of the Week
  • MidAmerican Energy and Page County, IowaWrite the Ordinance Into the Deal: The amendments Page County passed this week were included in a memorandum of understanding between the county and MidAmerican for the roughly 400-acre Nodaway Valley Solar Project. Supporters at the hearing argued for tax revenue and landowner income, and said decommissioning bonds keep the county in control. The play is to negotiate the ordinance language and the project terms in the same document, so one vote settles both.
  • Katie Singer, via Resilience.orgThe Conditions Checklist Travels: A guide circulating this week tells residents what to demand when solar or batteries are proposed. It includes a professional engineer's sealed hazard report, outside liability insurance instead of self-insurance, a decommissioning bond, written water answers, PFAS soil testing, a glare study, a fire chief's plan, and independent consultants paid for by the developer. Expect these items to show up word for word in public comment. Have the answers ready before the hearing.
  • Town of Edenton, N.C.Sixty Days, by Design: The town council unanimously approved a 60-day data center moratorium on Nov. 10, running through Jan. 10. It picked 60 days partly because shorter pauses need only seven days' public notice, instead of at least two weeks for longer ones. Members said they favor extending it up to a year, and staff will use the time to study electricity capacity. Chowan County holds a hearing on a one-year pause Nov. 23. A short pause on short notice now leads to a longer one later.

If the decision is going to move up a level, make sure your record goes with it.
