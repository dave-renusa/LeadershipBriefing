RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 67 · Wednesday, November 4, 2026

Good morning, team.

The returns are in, and the clearest lesson for our clients comes from Kansas. In both Emporia and El Dorado, voters banned grid-scale batteries on the same ballot as data centers. Neither petition was really aimed at storage. Storage was simply attached to a data center fight that organizers knew they could win. Madison County, Illinois sent Springfield a three-to-one message on local control, and the early count in Solano County, California was running against a new tax on wind, solar and batteries. Today's Deep Dive goes through the results, and the rest of the issue picks up where the ballots left off: a Minnesota solar permit granted over a judge's recommendation, a 5th Circuit ruling on who can connect to Texas's grid, and two utility commissions that changed hands.

Deep Dive

Batteries Not Included

Emporia, Kansas passed both of its citizen-initiated bans on Tuesday. According to election-night returns, Question 3, which bans Tier 2 and Tier 3 battery energy storage systems, passed 4,067 to 3,117. The data center ban did better, 4,346 to 2,891. Both ordinances run for ten years. Neither is settled law yet. Senior Judge Glen Braun still has to decide whether the petitions were legislative or administrative, and KVOE reports that if he finds them administrative they would most likely be void. Jay Vehige of Emporia Neighbors United said the result shows "a new generation in politics is here." On the other side, Emporians For Progress warned about the city "being closed for business."

El Dorado passed a combined measure by a larger margin. Voters approved a ten-year ban on high-impact data centers and Tier 2 and Tier 3 battery systems 2,965 to 1,183, more than two to one. The city had gone to court to keep the question off the ballot and lost in August. The judge's ruling left commissioners two choices: adopt the ordinance themselves or put it to voters. In both towns, petitioners wrote batteries into a data center question, and storage passed with the larger measure.

In Madison County, Illinois, the advisory question asking the General Assembly to restore local zoning control over wind and solar passed 58,799 to 18,657, or 75.9 percent. The county has no wind applications pending. The vote changes no statute, but legislators who want to reopen the 2023 siting law now have a number from a large Metro East county to point to. In Solano County, California, early returns on Measure E, which would raise the county's tax on wind and add new taxes on commercial solar, batteries and data centers, showed 63.6 percent voting no, 55,315 to 31,644. In this case voters chose not to load new costs onto the projects.

Data centers drew the most votes against them overall. Ballotpedia counted 41 local data center measures in 10 states, 28 of which would restrict development. In Ohio, residents of 18 municipalities voted on 21 measures, and Fortune reports that at least 14 passed bans or restrictions. Trenton's ban passed with about 80 percent. In Wilmington, about 80 percent rejected the rezonings for a data center project across four separate referendums. Ashville voted to block a campus that is already under construction.

The Takeaway

Kansas showed a new opposition tactic: attach batteries to a data center ban and let the larger fight carry them through. Neither town held a separate debate on storage, and neither had a storage project of the kind voters were asked to ban. If a client's battery project is in a town where data center opposition is organizing, the case for storage needs to be made separately and early. That means fire-department sign-off, the EPRI failure data, and the reliability argument, and ideally a council that defines and regulates battery tiers before a petition gets the chance to define them. Madison County carries a similar warning in a different form: once a question about local control reaches the ballot, it usually wins by a wide margin. The way to compete is to make sure our side helps decide what the question says.

Also On Our Radar
  • Free Lane in Texas: The 5th Circuit rejected GH America Energy's appeal after ERCOT canceled the interconnection application for its Blue Star Solar project in Val Verde County under the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act. The court said Texas has a "free lane" to restrict grid access for foreign-owned companies, and that federal CFIUS review does not preempt the state law. The developer, a subsidiary of a Chinese company, says the cancellation forced it to sell two other Texas projects at a loss. One attorney expects other states to take notice.
  • Six Hundred of the Target: Illinois's first storage procurement under its 2025 law awarded 600 megawatts, less than two-thirds of the round's goal. ComEd's territory sought 588 megawatts and signed one 80-megawatt project. Ameren Illinois signed three developers for 520 megawatts. Industry representatives blamed the stand-alone-only eligibility rule and long PJM interconnection delays, which one said led to "some cold feet." The state's target is 3 gigawatts by 2030.
  • Seventy Billion, Renewables First: Xcel Energy unveiled a $70 billion five-year capital plan that includes 9.2 gigawatts of renewables, 2.3 gigawatts of storage and 3.3 gigawatts of gas. About $7 billion is tied to contracted data center projects. CEO Bob Frenzel said, "existing customers should not subsidize new large loads," a line clients can use when opponents argue that renewables drive up rates.
  • Ninety-Seven Percent Safer: Canary Media, using EPRI's incident database, reports that the annual failure rate for U.S. grid batteries fell more than 97 percent from 2016 to 2025 while cumulative deployments grew about 250-fold. Batteries now fail at roughly the same rate as oil-filled transformers. Three of the past ten years had no known failures. Any client facing a storage petition should have these numbers ready.
  • Twenty-Six Gigawatts in the Queue: FirstEnergy says its data center pipeline reached 26 gigawatts in the third quarter. Utilities are also raising their spending plans, including AEP, which increased its investment plan 30 percent on generation and transmission.
On the Frontlines
  • MNOver the Judge's Objection: The Minnesota PUC granted a site permit for the 180-megawatt Lemon Hill Solar project in Olmsted County's Haverhill and Viola townships, even though an administrative law judge had recommended denial over farmland and legal requirements. The permit adds conditions on karst geology, well protection, water testing and visual screening. One commissioner acknowledged the project "has generated strong feelings in the community."
  • NJUtility-Owned Batteries: Exelon says its utilities could spend up to $8 billion on regulated storage in New Jersey, Maryland and Delaware. That includes a $1 billion, 500-megawatt Atlantic City Electric project with Invenergy, which the BPU is expected to decide late in the first quarter.
  • VTWind From the North: Gov. Phil Scott and Nova Scotia Premier Tim Houston signed a three-year agreement to explore bringing Nova Scotia offshore wind to New England through Vermont. The state now has to decide whether its nearly $1 billion upgrade of the 1985 Highgate Converter should carry two or three times as much power.
  • MABogs and Panels: Bogs in Kingston and Plymouth harvested under operating solar arrays for a second season. The Kingston site pairs 4.4 megawatts with a 13.5-megawatt-hour battery. Because cranberry bogs are protected wetlands, the land can never be developed, which takes away the most common local objection to solar on farmland.
  • NYLowville's Turn: The Lowville town board holds a public hearing tonight on a temporary moratorium on battery storage facilities, which would add it to the string of upstate New York towns that have paused storage this fall.
  • GAHubbard Holds: Democrat Peter Hubbard won reelection to the Public Service Commission over Republican Fitz Johnson. With about 90 percent counted, the AP had him ahead 56 to 43 percent, and Yahoo's AP coverage reports that Democrats now control Georgia Power's regulator for the first time in 31 years.
  • AZCommission in Play: Democrats Clara Pratte and Jonathon Hill led incumbent Republican Kevin Thompson and Rep. Ralph Heap on election night for two seats on the all-Republican Corporation Commission, in a race dominated by utility bills and data center growth.
  • OHSunbury Draws a Line: Sunbury voters passed a charter amendment banning data centers of 25 megawatts or more, aimed at a possible Amazon hyperscale project. Pataskala also adopted a charter ban.

In Kansas, batteries passed on a data center question because nobody stood up for storage as its own issue. Where a client has a storage project, make sure someone does.
