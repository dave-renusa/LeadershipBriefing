RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 59 · Friday, October 9, 2026

Friday morning, team.

The sharpest line of the week came from a planning document, not a vote. Montgomery County, Indiana's commissioners rejected Engie's 200-megawatt solar farm 3-0, and the county has no solar ban. What it has is a 2024 comprehensive plan that lists utility-scale solar and wind as undesirable land uses, in the same company as junkyards and landfills. Meanwhile, the Senate's four lead negotiators struck a permitting deal that won't get a vote until after the midterms, Rome, New York turned a battery moratorium into a permanent ban, and Texas landowners' rehearing motions on a 390-mile 765-kV line are on a clock that runs out Oct. 22. Whatever is written down early is what gets enforced late.

Also On Our Radar
  • On the List With the Junkyards: Montgomery County, Indiana's Board of Commissioners voted 3-0 on Sept. 28 to reject Engie's 200-megawatt solar project, which the company said would bring more than $300 million in construction spending and more than $40 million in local taxes. Commissioners cited testimony about property values and the area's character. The county's 2024 comprehensive plan, drawn from resident focus groups, lists utility-scale wind and solar as undesirable uses alongside junkyards, landfills and adult-oriented businesses. The plan commission had already recommended against the rezoning of more than 2,600 acres. A 2023 Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory study found small property-value effects, and the Sabin Center counts restrictions in more than 30 of Indiana's 92 counties.
  • The Deal That Waits: The Senate's four lead energy and environment negotiators, Republicans Shelley Moore Capito and Mike Lee and Democrats Martin Heinrich and Sheldon Whitehouse, reached a bipartisan permitting deal. It eases siting of interstate transmission lines, strengthens FERC's review role, limits legal challenges under NEPA, and requires data centers to pay their associated transmission costs. There is no vote before the midterms and Lee expects approval in the November lame-duck session. Whitehouse says Democrats still want clarity on how the bill would stop the administration from blocking wind and solar, and a Conservation Law Foundation attorney called the package a wish-list for fossil fuel interests.
  • Fifty-Five Days: Landowner group American Stewards of Liberty and other parties filed rehearing motions on the PUC of Texas's Aug. 28 approval of Oncor's Dinosaur-to-Drill Hole 765-kV line, about 390 miles in two segments. Under state law the motions are overruled if the commission does not act within 55 days, which is Oct. 22, and it can extend that to 100 days. The group argues that the 2023 law behind the Permian reliability study did not authorize a 765-kV buildout, and that landowners learned of route changes too late to object. Oncor says it held the required public meetings. The commission approved this line and another that day over landowner objections.
  • Pause, Then Prohibit: Rome, New York's Common Council voted 5-0 on Aug. 26 for Ordinance 9884, which bars battery energy storage systems anywhere in the city, two months after a six-month moratorium passed at the end of June. DLD Energy had bought 600 Canal St. to build one and its CEO says the company has already spent heavily on the site. The ban may block the project.
  • Smoke Again at Moss Landing: A new fire broke out at Vistra's Moss Landing battery plant on Sept. 18, during decommissioning, in batteries damaged in the January 2025 blaze. Workers have removed 34,000 modules and about 5,000 remain. Monterey County said the fire was not comparable to the 2025 one and issued shelter-in-place orders. Canary Media notes the 2020 plant's single-hall design is out of step with today's modular containers. Every storage hearing this winter will be held in its shadow.
  • Last Blade, Round the Bend: The 65th and final turbine at Revolution Wind went up Sept. 18. The project off Rhode Island has been powering homes and businesses since March and is expected to reach its full 704 megawatts later this year, after an administration stop-work order in August 2025.
  • Swan Song for the Skeptics: Clearway closed roughly $1.5 billion in financing for its 650-megawatt Swan Energy Center in Missouri and started construction, per Renewables Now. The financing is one more sign of how much capital is still moving into utility-scale solar.
  • Mesa View Money: Linea Energy closed financing for the 250-megawatt, 500-megawatt-hour Mesa View battery in Upton County, Texas. NORD/LB led a construction-to-term loan, tax equity bridge and letter-of-credit package with Texas Capital in the syndicate, and DESRI committed preferred equity. Dollar amounts weren't disclosed and commercial operation is scheduled for 2027.
On the Frontlines
  • INMeta's Mine Site: Zelestra closed a $350 million green credit facility for its 203-megawatt Reclamation Solar project on former coal-mining land in Gibson County. CIBC, BBVA and Société Générale are the lenders, Meta holds the power purchase agreement, the project is under construction, and commercial operation is expected at the end of 2027.
  • ILTwo Counties, Two Pauses: The McHenry County Board's committee advanced a 12-month data center moratorium for unincorporated areas, with a full board vote in November after the Nov. 3 election, even though no one is currently trying to build one. Neighboring Lake County approved an eight-month pause that runs to May 11, 2027 unless rules arrive sooner.
  • SCSixty-Five and Counting: Spartanburg County Council voted 5-1 on Sept. 21 for a one-year freeze on data centers larger than 65 megawatts, exempting ones already approved. Smaller facilities may build in industrial parks if they can't generate their own power. Residents argued the threshold is too high.
  • MOBackdoor Zoning: Cedar County's three commissioners imposed a 12-month moratorium on Sept. 8 on large solar, wind, battery-storage and data-center projects outside city limits, without a public vote and with no project proposed. A local business owner calls it backdoor zoning, and state law requires voter approval before a third-class county adopts zoning. No court has ruled on it.
  • WIThe County as Landlord: The Vernon County Board created a five-member special committee on Sept. 17 to study leasing part of the county farm for utility-scale solar, after a University of Wisconsin Extension siting program raised the idea. An earlier proposal covered a 15-megawatt, 85-acre project.
  • OKQuiet in Lincoln County: Apex Clean Energy's roughly 35,000-acre, up-to-400-megawatt Sandstone Hills wind project is reportedly on hold, according to a state representative. Apex may decide by the first quarter of 2027 whether to abandon it and is closing its local office.
  • INOne Million to Sign: Frankfort approved a community enhancement agreement tied to a $7 billion hyperscale data center, requiring a $1 million payment within 30 days, per Heatmap. Two more city council votes are needed before construction can begin.
  • TXPause the Wires: A Texas state senator has urged Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins to back a statewide pause on transmission approvals. As of Heatmap's report, she had not.
  • TXReview With Exemptions: The state NAACP chapter called Gov. Greg Abbott's statewide data center review pointless because many projects are exempt. Attorney General Ken Paxton, a U.S. Senate candidate, supports continued data center growth with conditions.
  • GASolar First, Committee Second: Bulloch County commissioners voted unanimously Oct. 6 to start preliminary engineering on a roughly $2.5 million solar system for the sheriff's office expansion and correctional campus, with a final agreement to return. The same meeting created a 15-member data center committee on a 3-1 vote.
Plays of the Week
  • ZelestraBuild on Land Already Spent: Putting 203 megawatts of solar on former coal-mining ground with Meta as the buyer sidesteps the farmland argument that sank Montgomery County's project the same week.
  • Cumberland County, TNRegulate What You Can't Block: Commissioners voted 16-0 in May for an Energy Siting Agreement with Silicon Ranch after concluding the county lacked authority to stop the project. It secured $230,000 in community benefit fees plus construction requirements.
  • Montgomery County CommissionersWrite It Into the Plan: The county has no solar ordinance to defend. Its 2024 comprehensive plan, shaped by resident focus groups, did the work, and the denial followed the document. Check what each client county's plan says before the application is filed.
  • Rome Common CouncilSequence the Ban: A six-month moratorium, a planning board recommendation, then a 5-0 prohibition gave the council a clean record at each step. The developer's counter-argument is that he applied before the ban and should be grandfathered.

The record gets written before the vote. Montgomery County's plan, Rome's three-step ordinance and Cedar County's order all show it. For each active file, read the county's comprehensive plan and ask what it already says about our technology.
