RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 61 · Friday, October 16, 2026

Good morning, team.

The week's sharpest sentence came from a county board member who voted yes. In Will County, Illinois, members voted down one solar farm and passed another while saying out loud that state law leaves them little choice and that a no mostly buys a lawsuit. Upstate, New York's siting office approved roughly 800 acres of solar without the towns at the table, and in Peoria County a zoning board voted 4-2 against 113 turbines that the full county board will now decide. Local officials are increasingly saying which rooms they wish they were still in. The Justice Department, meanwhile, asked a federal court to reopen the permit for an offshore wind farm that is already making power.

Also On Our Radar
  • Begging for Help in Will County: The Will County Board rejected US Solar's 4-megawatt project near Monee 11-8 with three abstentions, after the fire district cited vegetation fire risk and township officials cited the neighboring subdivision and stormwater. It then approved Arena Renewables' two 5-megawatt plants on Spencer Road near Manhattan on a party-line 12-11 vote, and postponed Cultivate Power's 5-megawatt solar-plus-battery project on Baker Road to November after Manhattan Township voted 4-0 to object. The planning commission and land use committee had recommended denying all three. Members said they dislike the state law that overrides local control but fear the litigation cost of voting no.
  • Four Creeks, Four to Two: Peoria County's Zoning Board of Appeals voted 4-2 against a special use permit for Repsol Renewables' Four Creeks Wind, about 113 turbines and up to 663 megawatts in the county's northwest. The recommendation goes to a Land Use Committee special meeting on Nov. 5 and then to the full County Board. Opponents cited noise, wildlife and rural character; supporters cited school and local tax revenue.
  • The Line Without a Route: Campbell County became the eighth locality to formally oppose the 115-mile Valley Link transmission line from Campbell County to Culpeper County, Virginia. The Dominion, AEP and FirstEnergy partnership has not picked a route; it will file several options with the State Corporation Commission, which can take up to a year to rule. Construction would start in 2028 and finish around 2030.
  • Grove Gets a Battery Vote: The James City County Planning Commission voted 5-2 to recommend approval of Tenaska's 650-megawatt Pelican Energy Storage on the former BASF acrylic fiber plant site near Virginia's Grove neighborhood. Supporters cite $90 million in tax revenue over 30 years; opponents say Grove has carried decades of industry, and Tenaska has not finished an emergency response plan. The Board of Supervisors takes it up in November.
  • Tax Break Denied, Towers Going Up Anyway: The Madison County, New York Industrial Development Agency on Oct. 14 rejected a $30 million, 20-year payment-in-lieu-of-taxes request for Liberty Renewables' Hoffman Falls Wind because host community agreements with Eaton, Nelson, Fenner and Smithfield had not been signed. Liberty says construction is already under way and will continue, and points to $1 million in electric bill savings and at least $1 million in grants.
  • Veto Under Review: Lexington's mayor vetoed a solar zoning ordinance, passed 8-7 on Sept. 10, that capped large solar at 1 percent of the county's land. This week the Kentucky Solar Industries Association asked the council to investigate, citing a 1991 Kentucky Supreme Court decision it says leaves no room for a mayoral veto of a zoning change. The mayor's office declined to comment.
  • Remand Request, Round Three: The Justice Department on Oct. 9 asked a federal judge to send the Interior Department's approval of Revolution Wind back for reconsideration, in a case brought by the anti-wind group Green Oceans. The 704-megawatt project off Rhode Island has been generating since March, and its last turbine went up in September. Interior says it is complying with a briefing schedule, not announcing a policy change. A court rejected earlier attempts in 2025.
On the Frontlines
  • VAStafford Steps Back: Stafford County canceled the Oct. 14 Planning Commission hearing on four data center applications spanning more than 1,500 acres after staff recommended denying most of them. The hearing is now set for Nov. 18.
  • NYWatertown, Without the Town: The state Office of Renewable Energy Siting granted full permits to AES for a solar project covering about 800 acres around Old Rome State Road in Watertown and Hounsfield. Residents are organizing ahead of a Hounsfield hearing, some hoping to take legal action against the permit.
  • WISandhill Pushback: Opponents of MN8 Energy's roughly 1,039-acre Sandhill Solar in Barron County met Oct. 10 and plan to intervene before the Public Service Commission through Save Barron County Farms Inc. They argue school districts lose tax base because Wisconsin exempts utility-scale solar from local property tax. No formal application has been filed.
  • VAWashington County Writes Storage Rules: Washington County supervisors unanimously adopted a zoning ordinance limiting where battery energy storage facilities can be built, effective immediately and applying to Tier 2 facilities.
  • INKnox Adds Distance: Knox County commissioners approved a revised solar ordinance adding soil and water monitoring and an extra 100 feet of setback from a home, or 500 feet when homes sit on more than one side. It now goes to the Area Plan Commission.
  • NYSchuyler Turbines Draw a Crowd: A proposal for 14 to 20 turbines across about 250 acres in eastern Hector and Catharine is drawing health, wildlife and watershed concerns, while the developer points to an estimated $21.8 million in property tax revenue over 30 years.
  • PARogue's Wind Dedicated: Competitive Power Ventures and Cambria County officials dedicated the 114-megawatt, 19-turbine Rogue's Wind across about 6,200 acres of Cambria and Clearfield counties, operating since July. CPV presented $20,000 to a firefighters' tuition program.
  • ILHalf a Gigawatt Short: The Illinois Power Agency is seeking stakeholder feedback after its summer storage procurement came in 438 megawatts under target. Downstate MISO territory beat its goal; ComEd territory bought 80 megawatts against a 588-megawatt ask, per mGrid's tally.
  • NVWashoe's 450 Megawatts: The Washoe County Board of Commissioners had a resolution on its Oct. 13 agenda to begin amending the regional plan's utility corridor map for a project of up to 450 megawatts of solar, a 200-megawatt battery and a 345-kilovolt substation.
  • AZBlack Mesa Waits on FERC: Nature and People First's proposed pumped-storage plant on the Navajo Nation awaits a FERC preliminary permit decision. FERC denied three earlier Black Mesa permits in 2024 for lack of Navajo consent and is considering rolling that policy back at the administration's request.
Plays of the Week
  • Madison County IDAHost Agreement First: The agency made signed host community agreements a condition of the tax-break hearing, then denied the $30 million request when they were missing. The developer is building anyway, which shows the limit of that leverage. Get the town agreements signed before any incentive vote.
  • Kentucky Solar Industries AssociationCite the Procedure: Rather than argue solar's merits, the trade group's letter argues state zoning law allows no mayoral veto, resting on a 1991 Kentucky Supreme Court decision. A procedural challenge can undo a restrictive ordinance without a public fight over the technology.
  • Competitive Power VenturesHold the Ceremony Locally: CPV's Rogue's Wind dedication put a county commissioner, a conservation authority and a firefighters' association on the program at a recreation area the project is tied to, with a $20,000 check for firefighter tuition. The benefit was visible and local.
  • Nature and People FirstNever Pay for Silence: The developer's 2024 offer of $10,000 to a Navajo nonprofit, contingent on ending its opposition and issuing a supporting statement, is now a news story. A benefit that is conditioned on silence reads as a bribe once it surfaces.

Will County members said the quiet part out loud: a no that loses in court is a bill for the county. Before a client's hearing, find out what the board thinks it can legally say, and build the record that lets it say yes.
