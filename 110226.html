RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 66 · Monday, November 2, 2026

Happy Monday, all.

Tomorrow, voters decide siting questions directly. Madison County, Illinois asks whether Springfield should give wind and solar zoning back to counties. Jay, Maine votes on a 180-day pause that would land on what its developer calls the largest solar project in the state. Solano County, California votes on a new tax on solar and batteries and a higher one on wind. Emporia, Kansas votes on banning larger batteries outright. All four questions got onto the ballot because a board or a petition drive put them there, which means each one had organizers who wrote the language, gathered the signatures and set the terms of the debate long before Election Day. After the votes, the work goes back to county boards. Peoria County takes up a 113-turbine wind farm on Thursday after its zoning board recommended denial, and Seward County, Kansas has just published a draft permit for its part of a 1,200-megawatt wind project.

Must Read

Siting at the Polls

Madison County, Illinois voters will answer an advisory question tomorrow: "Shall the Illinois General Assembly restore zoning control of wind and solar farm developments to local governments instead of mandating them from the state level?" The county has no pending wind applications. The question is aimed at the 2023 state law that limits how far counties can restrict wind and solar. The result will not change any statute, but legislators who want to reopen that law will be able to cite it.

In Jay, Maine, residents petitioned a 180-day moratorium on new commercial ground-mounted solar onto the ballot. They gathered 534 valid signatures over a Friday and Saturday, where 226 were needed. The Select Board then voted 3-0 to send it to voters. The target is Walden Renewables' Alden Mill Solar, which the company says could reach 150 megawatts on 800 to 900 acres, making it the largest solar project in Maine. Much of it would sit on the former Androscoggin Mill land. Walden says it will keep working on the project until the vote. The petition's organizer says his group is not against solar.

Solano County, California's Measure E is a tax rather than a pause. It would raise the county's tax on wind energy from $0.00003 to $0.00008 per kilowatt-hour and apply the same new rate to commercial solar. It would also add $1.50 per megawatt-hour of approved battery capacity and $5 per square foot a year on data centers, even though the unincorporated county has none today. Supervisors put it on the ballot 4-0, and the county estimates it would raise about $353,000 a year. In Emporia, Kansas, residents petitioned two ordinances onto the ballot. Question 3 would ban Tier 2 and Tier 3 battery storage systems citywide, and Question 2 would ban high-impact data centers. The city is still fighting the data center petition in court and says a yes vote cannot take effect until that case is resolved.

Data centers are on other ballots too. Several small Maine towns are voting on data center bans, including Bethel and North Berwick. In Ashville, Ohio, voters decide whether to repeal a development agreement that exempted two EdgeConneX data centers under construction from the village moratorium. Repeal would not stop construction. In South Carolina's 4th District, a Democrat who lives about a mile from a data center under construction is challenging Rep. William Timmons, who says he has no role in siting. The last vote of this kind was in Richland County, Ohio in May, where voters narrowly kept a ban on most wind and solar, the first time residents had forced a referendum on one.

The Takeaway

Once a siting question goes to voters, the campaign effectively becomes the hearing. It runs mostly in coffee shops and on social media, with no staff report and no developer presentation to answer it. The organizers in Jay gathered more than twice the signatures they needed in two days. In Richland County, the ban survived even though its opponents had forced the vote. For any client project in a county or town that allows citizen initiatives, the petition stage is the real point of decision. Identify the local allies, the farmers and the fire chief early, and have them ready before a petition starts circulating, not after it is certified.

Also On Our Radar
  • "Scrap the Whole Damn Project": Oncor met landowners in Stephenville on Oct. 26 about its two 765-kV routes that Texas regulators have approved, Dinosaur-Longshore and Longshore-Drill Hole. The company estimates about 800 affected landowners. The lines could use steel lattice towers about 15 stories tall on a 200-foot right-of-way. Landowners can ask for route changes, but each change can bring in new landowners or add towers. It was Oncor's ninth meeting since Oct. 12, and the tenth is Nov. 4 in De Leon. One landowner said her solution was to "scrap the whole damn project," and another said, "We feel like we're being railroaded."
  • Approved Under Duress: The Hadley, Massachusetts Planning Board voted 4-0 to approve a 5-megawatt battery in a gravel pit inside the town's aquifer protection district, nearly two years after it rejected an earlier plan. The developer had sued over that rejection. Members said the project is grandfathered under older zoning, and that the state's revised Dover Amendment rules undercut three of their four original grounds for denial. One member said he voted yes "under duress." Neighbors said they found out about the project on social media and asked the board to reopen the hearing. Town counsel said the board had no basis to reopen it.
  • Thirty Percent More Batteries: BloombergNEF raised its U.S. storage forecast by 30 percent over the next decade. It now projects 213.9 gigawatts installed by 2030 and 434.4 gigawatts by 2036. Batteries at data centers alone are expected to grow from 500 megawatts in 2025 to 4.9 gigawatts this year. The combined duty on stationary batteries from mainland China is 40.9 percent.
  • Review Before Rebar: Pine Island, Minnesota approved a settlement with the Minnesota Center for Environmental Advocacy that sends Google's hyperscale Project Skyway to a new environmental review supervised by the state Pollution Control Agency, the first state-agency review of a data center in Minnesota. Google and developer Ryan Companies must disclose water and energy use and present a mitigation plan, and construction cannot begin until both the city and the agency approve it. The Minnesota attorney general's office has warned that Google's power deal with Xcel could shift large costs onto other customers.
  • DeSoto Twice Denied: DeSoto County, Florida commissioners deadlocked 2-2 on Oct. 27, which killed a rezoning of about 238 acres for a hyperscale data center. One commissioner recused. The next night they turned down a second, larger request. DCIP Group has sought about 826 acres near Arcadia for a complex of data centers powered by natural gas.
On the Frontlines
  • ILPeoria's Thursday Vote: The Peoria County Board is expected to decide on Nov. 5 whether to grant a special use permit for Four Creeks Wind, up to 113 turbines in the county's northwest. The zoning board of appeals voted 4-2 to recommend denial.
  • KSThresher's Draft: Seward County released a 78-page draft permit on Oct. 29 for Invenergy's wind project and is asking for public input. Commissioners will begin deliberating no sooner than Nov. 16. Thresher Wind is a proposed 1,200-megawatt project spanning Meade, Seward and Gray counties.
  • ARA Gigawatt-Hour Inside the Fence: Entergy Arkansas planned to file around Oct. 19 for a 1,000-megawatt-hour battery on just under 10 acres inside the fence of its 250-megawatt Driver Solar site near Osceola. If approved, construction would start in the second quarter of 2027 and finish a year later.
  • NYShelby Sets the Date: After its planning board recommended a one-year battery storage moratorium, the Shelby town board set a public hearing for Nov. 10.
  • MNGopher Gets a Coalition: Farmers and landowners in southeast Minnesota organized against the Gopher to Badger line, a 765-kV and 161-kV double-circuit project from Xcel and Dairyland that connects to the Wisconsin fights over the MariBell and BECI lines.
  • USUpgrades Only: While permitting talks with Democrats continue, the administration says it will clear wind projects through routine airspace approvals, as a court order requires. So far, Heatmap reports, only upgrades to existing wind farms have been approved and no new wind farms have moved forward.
  • OHNo Duty to Review: The Ohio Supreme Court ruled unanimously that Lordstown may stop reviewing a site plan for four three-story data centers that Bristolville 25 Developer LLC filed days before the village adopted its moratorium. The court said the village is "under no legal duty to proceed."
  • KYGeorgetown Goes to June: Georgetown's council voted unanimously to extend its data center moratorium a second time, to June 30, 2027, and began drafting a ban on hyperscale data centers.
  • AZSeven Hundred Megawatts of Gas: Glendale approved annexing and rezoning 160 acres for Project Eagle, a $36 billion data center campus about two miles from Luke Air Force Base. The campus would run on about 700 megawatts of natural gas generators. Residents raised groundwater and air quality.
  • AZPima Tightens the Permit: Pima County's planning and zoning commission voted 8-1 to require a Type 3 conditional use permit for data centers in the CI-2 zone, about 2 percent of county land, along with tighter water, noise and monitoring rules. Supervisors take it up in early December.

Every question on tomorrow's ballots was written by someone months ago. On the next one, make sure a client's allies are part of drafting the question, not just campaigning for or against it once it is printed.
