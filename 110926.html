Volume 69 · Monday, November 9, 2026

Happy Monday, all.

A denial is only as strong as its record, and this week's clearest lesson is that the record gets tested in court. Indiana's Tamarack Solar has appealed a Marshall County decision that a judge upheld, and the county expects the case could run into next summer. Meanwhile, Webb County, Texas has three renewable energy zones on its commission agenda today, and New York towns keep adding battery pauses. The rest of the issue shows where the next fights are forming, from offshore wind in federal court to Kansas and Iowa wind projects at the planning-board level.

Must Read

Appeal Ready: A Denial Goes Up One More Level

Tamarack Solar is appealing a Marshall County, Indiana court ruling that upheld the county's rejection of an industrial solar farm. The county received the notice of appeal on October 29, and officials expect the case could run into next summer. The appeal sits on top of a Board of Zoning Appeals decision, so the question is whether the local record and the ordinance language held up under review. Read the coverage of the appeal.

The Takeaway

Indiana shows the other half of the denial playbook. Once a county says no, the fight shifts to whether that no was written down with specific reasons the court can defend. Any client facing a local denial should assume the appeal will be decided on the record, not on the public comment. Build the findings, the staff reports and the ordinance citations as if a judge will read them line by line, because one will.

Also On Our Radar
  • Remand Request: The Trump administration asked a court to send the Maryland Offshore Wind approval back to the agency, a move that would open the door to revoking a permit for a wind farm already supplying power to the grid. The suit was brought by Ocean City. The request was reported on October 29, and it is a federal-permit precedent every offshore developer will read closely.
  • Three Zones on the Agenda: Webb County, Texas commissioners are set to consider three renewable energy zones at today's meeting. The zones would cover about 23,000 acres and include public hearings on two proposed wind projects and one solar project.
  • Board Says No, Commission Gets the Call: Dickinson County, Kansas planning and zoning voted 6-1 to deny Enel Green Power's conditional use permit for the Hope Ridge wind project. The county commission was scheduled to take up the request on November 7, so the board's recommendation now sits in front of the elected body.
  • One Year, No Batteries: North Elba, New York approved a one-year moratorium on battery energy storage systems, joining a growing list of Adirondack communities holding off on storage this fall.
  • Backdoor Zoning: Cedar County, Missouri commissioners paused solar, wind and data center projects for one year. Commissioners cited due diligence, residents cited a lack of public input, and one business owner called the pause "backdoor zoning."
  • Ringgold's Border Watch: Taylor County, Iowa posted a public hearing notice for the proposed Black Maple Wind Project, which would include up to 110 turbine locations. Taylor County borders Ringgold County, where the Ringgold Wind project is proposed, so any ordinance or hearing language in Taylor is early warning for our Ringgold work.
On the Frontlines
  • WAHarvest Hills Heads to the State: After Whitman County's March rules, the developer says the county ordinance eliminates locally permitted wind, so Harvest Hills Wind is seeking state approval for its plans on the Palouse. A Nez Perce group has publicly backed the project.
  • ILLament and Land Use: Will County's board rejected a solar farm near Monee and approved one near Manhattan, while members lamented the lack of local control over the outcome.
  • MIConflict Waived, Project Moves: Wexford County commissioners approved a conflict-of-interest waiver tied to the Shipyard solar project on Nov. 4. The project itself falls under the joint planning commission, not the county board.
  • NYSix Months, Battery Pause: Huntington's officials approved a six-month battery storage moratorium on August 9.
  • AZOre Town Clears: Pinal County approved zoning for a 145-MW solar project and battery storage system at Ore Town on Aug. 5, after the item was continued from July 1.
  • AZWhetstone Says No: Cochise County's planning and zoning commission unanimously rejected a solar expansion that would have roughly doubled a facility near Whetstone and added two battery storage sites.

Build the receipts before the rezoning, and write the terms before the vote.
