RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 65 · Friday, October 30, 2026

Friday morning, team.

This week's fights came down to a distance. Wyoming approved a 139-turbine wind farm that Laramie County had rejected, then attached a 2.5-mile setback from occupied homes that began as a condition for one property and grew to cover the whole project boundary during the hearing. In Vernon Parish, homeowners asked for a half-mile and kept 500 feet. In Page County, the number on the table is 300. Dearborn County, Indiana is arguing about acres instead, with proposals ranging from 200 to 1,200. Each of those numbers will be quoted in the next county over. Whoever is in the room when one gets written is setting terms for projects that have not been proposed yet.

Also On Our Radar
  • Vernon Lifts the Pause: The Vernon Parish Police Jury voted unanimously on Oct. 23 to lift its solar moratorium, clearing the way for Treaty Oak's solar farm of more than 10,000 acres along the Rapides Parish line near Pitkin, Louisiana. The jury also amended its solar ordinance: a 500-foot property-line setback that landowners can waive, 300 feet from public roads, native vegetative screening, at least one public community meeting before a parish permit, and decommissioning bond reviews every three years. Nearby homeowners had asked for a half-mile. Leesville business owners attended in support, and the jury president said the parish needs new industry as timber and Fort Polk decline.
  • Emmet Hits Pause: The Emmet County, Iowa Board of Supervisors unanimously approved a resolution imposing a temporary moratorium on utility-scale solar and battery storage in unincorporated areas. It runs through Dec. 31 while officials draft zoning ordinances.
  • Two and a Half Miles: Wyoming's Industrial Siting Division on Monday approved the state permit for the Laramie Range Wind Project, 139 turbines and 650 megawatts on 41,220 acres northwest of Cheyenne, with 26 conditions. Laramie County commissioners denied the project in September 2025, and developer ConnectGen, a Repsol affiliate, went to the state. One condition requires a 2.5-mile setback from occupied residences for turbines and certain other infrastructure. It was meant for one nearby property and was expanded to the entire project boundary during the hearing. The developer says it may change the layout.
  • On RWE's Dime: Ottawa County, Michigan commissioners voted 10-1 on Tuesday to seek intervenor status in the state's review of Silver Maple Solar, RWE's 200-megawatt, $330 million project on about 1,914 acres in Zeeland and Jamestown townships. They accepted $50,000 from RWE to pay for it, money Public Act 233 requires the developer to offer. The petition is due Nov. 12, a prehearing conference is set for Nov. 19, and an evidentiary hearing is likely in late January. The board chair says intervening does not mean the county supports or opposes the project.
  • The Permit Expires First: A jury trial over the Kansas Sky Energy Center was pushed to July 12-16, 2027, the Lawrence Journal-World reports. Douglas County's conditional use permit for the 159-megawatt solar farm expires April 13, 2027, three months earlier. Grant Township and neighboring property owners sued in 2024, and a temporary injunction is holding up construction.
  • Too Early for the Check: Cenergy Power offered Butte County, California up to $300,000 a year, land for a fire station and a $250,000 sheriff's grant for a 1,200-acre solar and battery project near Chico. Supervisors took no action, and one called the package "very premature" without an environmental review. The county cannot approve or deny the project, because the state has the final say. Residents raised blue oaks and battery fire risk. The company plans to file in November.
  • Rejected in September, Back in October: Arkansas regulators denied Entergy's request to buy power from the 440-megawatt Big Island solar plant in September, finding it had not shown reasonable costs or customer savings. Entergy has put the same plant in a Google-funded second phase for its West Memphis data center campus, about $600 million with 1.4 gigawatt-hours of batteries and two 230-kV lines. The commission will review the package.
  • Fire From the Left: Pima County Board of Supervisors Chair Jen Allen, a Democrat, says she cannot support Repsol's proposed solar farm near Three Points, Arizona, a 500-megawatt project with 250 megawatts of batteries on state trust land at an estimated $575 million. The Center for Biological Diversity also opposes it. The company plans to file permit applications with the county early next year.
On the Frontlines
  • COBig Bronco, No Bucking: Adams County commissioners approved Lightsource bp's Big Bronco Solar and Storage on Oct. 13: 300 megawatts of solar and up to 300 megawatts of batteries on 2,800 acres near Watkins, at $675 million. The vote was unanimous and no local opposition came up at hearings. One resident asked for a battery decommissioning bond. The project still has no power buyer.
  • IAThree Hundred Feet, First Reading: Page County supervisors held the first hearing on Tuesday on solar amendments tied to MidAmerican's 50-megawatt Nodaway Valley project, including a waivable 300-foot setback from non-participating homes. They took no action. The planning and zoning commission recommended keeping the 2024 ordinance. The second reading is Nov. 5.
  • INDearborn's Acre Auction: Dearborn County's Plan Commission discussed capping a single solar project at 200 acres per owner and solar on farmland at about 3 percent of the county, roughly 1,800 acres, against a draft that allows 1,200 acres and a citizens' proposal of 500. A motion to strip property-value protections failed 4-4-1. The planning director hopes for a vote Nov. 12.
  • ILFinal at the Zoning Board: Lake County's Zoning Board of Appeals approved a roughly 5-megawatt New Leaf Energy solar farm on 49 acres near Mundelein over objections from Hawthorn Woods, Long Grove and about 25 residents. On commercial solar permits, the board's decision is final and does not go to the county board.
  • TXLeave Us Alone: Dickens County commissioners on Oct. 13 unanimously rejected a Chapter 312 tax abatement for Timberlake Solar as residents in the room voiced opposition.
  • VADominion Prices the Mandate: Dominion's 2026 integrated resource plan, filed Oct. 15, adds 3.5 gigawatts of storage against a state target of 16,000 megawatts by 2045, alongside more than 21 gigawatts of solar and 3.4 gigawatts of offshore wind. It says a full-storage portfolio costs about $6.3 billion more over 20 years, or $15 to $16 a month on a residential bill.
  • MOTest Results First: Missouri regulators on Oct. 21 approved Ameren's 545 megawatts of batteries and 400 megawatts of solar at sites in four counties. At the 250-megawatt Millcreek site, Ameren must file confirmation that Megapack 3 safety testing is complete at least 90 days before batteries arrive. Who pays waits for a rate case.
  • MIFish Creek Flows: DTE Energy started commercial operations at the 132-megawatt Fish Creek Solar Park, PV Tech reports.
  • NYFour More Months: Ossining's village trustees referred a four-month extension of their battery moratorium to the planning board, which would move the end date from Dec. 27 to April 27, 2027.
  • VAStafford Staff Says No: Stafford County moved its Kings Highway data center hearings to Nov. 18. County staff have recommended denying most of the applications, including the 845-acre Forest Lane site, which staff says sits outside the county's preferred location for data centers.
  • ORAn Emergency Over a Pause: The Umatilla city council voted 5-1 for a state of emergency over Gov. Tina Kotek's statewide data center permit pause, citing lost tax revenue. The town hosts more than 20 data center projects, and the resolution authorizes the city manager to pursue legal remedies.
  • WVA Goal, Not a Mandate: West Virginia's Public Service Commission softened a rule under the state's data center law, concluding that a mandatory coal-plant capacity requirement would go beyond legislative intent.
Plays of the Week
  • Ottawa CountyNeutral, With a Lawyer: Commissioners joined the Silver Maple case without taking a position on the project, framing their role as telling regulators about impacts on farming, housing, water and the grid. They are paying for it with funding the law makes the developer offer. Expect more counties to read Public Act 233 this way. A county that is officially neutral is harder to answer than one that is opposed, so a client's host community agreement talks should start before the intervention deadline.
  • John Rich and Alex FasuloAim at the Agency: Rich, the Trump administration's special rural landowner envoy, joined anti-renewables activist Fasulo at a roundtable in Schuylerville, New York, where she called for eliminating the Office of Renewable Energy Siting. They teased "MAJOR things" in Upstate New York without details. Local opposition is now going after the state body that overrides it, with a federal title in the room.
  • Cenergy PowerThe Offer Before the Review: The developer brought a benefits package to Butte County before filing its state application and set aside nearly 600 acres for blue oaks in September. A supervisor still called it premature. Early is usually right, but a number offered ahead of the impact analysis gives a board a reason to wait, so present the package alongside the mitigation.
  • Entergy and GoogleChange the Payer: Regulators said customers had not been shown the savings on Big Island, so Entergy returned with Google covering the investment and a claim of at least $500 million in new customer savings. A project turned down on cost can come back with a different answer to who pays.

A condition written for one neighbor became a 2.5-mile rule for 41,000 acres before the hearing ended. Read every proposed condition for how far it could stretch, and object on the record while it is still small.
