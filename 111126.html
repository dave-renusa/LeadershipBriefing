RenUSA Leadership Brief

Volume 70 · Wednesday, November 11, 2026

Good morning, team.

The transmission fight now starts before there is a filing to fight. West Virginia shows it this week from both ends. One county has formally opposed a 500-kV line twice, and the state's hearings on that line are already over. Meanwhile, a 765-kV line with no preferred route yet is running twelve open houses across three states. The rest of the issue follows the same idea at a smaller scale. A battery that a Michigan township once rejected came back with its safety paperwork and won 4-1. An Illinois zoning board turned down a 300-megawatt battery and split on a 500-megawatt solar farm. The projects that win are the ones that answer the objections before the vote.

Deep Dive

Wired Twice: One West Virginia County Says No Again, and Another Line Comes Calling

The Preston County Commission adopted its second resolution opposing the Mid-Atlantic Resiliency Link at its meeting on Monday, Nov. 9. MARL is NextEra Energy Transmission's proposed 500-kV line through Monongalia and Preston counties in north-central West Virginia. The resolution says the proposed route "would significantly impact local landowners, farms, forests, and rural communities." The timing matters. The state Public Service Commission finished its MARL hearings on Nov. 2, and a decision is expected in March 2027. So the county is adding to the record after the testimony closed and before the commission rules.

The second line is earlier in the process and much larger. Valley Link is holding twelve drop-in open houses in November on Valley North, a 260-mile, 765-kV line that would run from Putnam County, West Virginia to Frederick County, Maryland. Nine of the meetings are in West Virginia between Nov. 4 and Nov. 19. The others are in Winchester on Nov. 5, Lovettsville tomorrow, Nov. 12, and Brunswick, Maryland on Nov. 19. The company says more than 6,500 people attended its summer round. It notified property owners within 1,000 feet of the potential routes, and also those whose land is no longer near one. PJM selected the project through its long-term planning process. Valley Link has given no date for a preferred route or a regulatory filing. Even so, residents in Kanawha County have already said they are building a coalition county by county.

The Takeaway

Opposition to transmission now organizes at the open-house stage and arrives at the commission as formal county resolutions. In this fight, a county resolution is the opposition's most effective tool. It costs nothing, it goes on the public record, and a regulator cannot easily dismiss it the way it can dismiss a comment card. A county that has passed one resolution can pass a second at any point before the decision. For any client with a line, a substation, or a project that depends on new wire, the work starts before the route is drawn. Know which commissioners will be asked to sign a resolution, and get to them first with the landowner notices, the tax figures, and a plain account of what is still undecided.

Also On Our Radar
  • Full Power, Still in Court: Revolution Wind is fully operational at 704 megawatts from 65 turbines, enough for about 350,000 homes. The Interior Department halted construction twice, in August and December 2025, and federal judges let work resume both times. The administration's bid to reevaluate the project's permit is still in court, so an operating wind farm remains a live legal target.
  • The Clock, Maxed Out: On Nov. 5, Texas regulators extended their time to the "maximum extent authorized by law" to consider rehearing motions on the Howard-to-Solstice line, a roughly 370-mile 765-kV route approved Sept. 11. Oncor's Dinosaur-to-Drill Hole segment got the same extension in October. Both lines are now on hold, which delays construction and the landowners' day in court by the same amount.
  • Solar Where the Coal Was: Panamint Capital closed the first phase of a $1.8 billion green loan for Big Rooter Power, a 1.2-gigawatt solar project at an active coal-mining site next to its Twin Oaks power station in Robertson County, Texas. Panamint calls it the largest brownfield renewable project in North America. A former energy site with an existing grid connection is the easiest local sell in this business.
  • Denied in June, 4-1 in October: Van Buren Township, Michigan planners voted 4-1 to recommend RWE's 200-MW/800-MWh Staghorn battery. The same commission had recommended denial in June over public-health and emergency-safety questions. The township board sent the project back for a fire-safety review. RWE returned with updated safety materials and sign-offs from the township's consultants and the fire marshal. That is the tactic to copy: get the local fire official on record before the vote. RWE's counsel also noted that if local approval fails, the project can go to the state Public Service Commission.
  • Second Thoughts in Idaho: Bannock County's planning council voted 5-0 to recommend a land-use rewrite that would end the county's 2024 ban on large-scale solar and wind and allow conditional-use permits instead. It added two conditions: future size caps, and more information on battery storage. Commissioners make the final call. It is a rare case of a county moving to reverse a ban, and other counties with bans will be watching.
On the Frontlines
  • ILOne No, One Draw: Tazewell County's Zoning Board of Appeals unanimously recommended denying Strata Clean Energy's 300-MW Lumina battery next to Ameren's Tazewell substation. It deadlocked, with one abstention, on the 500-MW, 3,400-acre Mackinaw Solar project. Residents raised water and property values. Both projects go to the Land Use Committee and then the full board.
  • INRules First, Project Later: Vigo County commissioners voted 2-1 to create a special exception for battery storage in the county zoning code. The rule change comes ahead of NextEra's proposed 350-MW, roughly $414 million Sandcut project, which still needs its own approvals. The dissenting commissioner's concern was a possible tax abatement, not safety.
  • ILNorth of Gilman, Forward: Iroquois County's planning and zoning committee approved a special-use permit for a roughly 190.9-MW solar facility north of Gilman. Conditions include a 40-year term, screening, and full decommissioning. The full county board still has to vote.
  • NMBonds for a Battery: Torrance County commissioners voted unanimously to declare their intent to issue up to $150 million in industrial revenue bonds for Britton Storage's 60-MW battery. The battery would sit on about 7.7 acres inside an existing solar facility's fence line. The intent vote did not approve the bonds themselves.
  • VASmaller, Not Yet Sold: King George County's planning commission voted unanimously to defer Gibson Solar's revised solar-and-livestock project. Supervisors denied the original in July 2025. The developer came back with a 640-acre footprint, but the commission set no new date.
  • IADubuque Hits Pause: Dubuque County supervisors sent their renewable-energy ordinance back for more study and agreed on a six-month moratorium on utility-scale permits. The zoning commission then aligned its work with the pause through June.
  • NYMarcellus Lines Up: The Town of Marcellus has set a Dec. 2 public hearing on a six-month battery storage moratorium. The proposal came to the board on Nov. 4, the latest in New York's run of storage pauses.
  • WABorder Town Pause: Blaine's city council voted 6-0 for a moratorium of up to one year on data centers and battery energy storage while staff write code language, putting large load and grid storage under the same pause.
